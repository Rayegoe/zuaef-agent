# Editorial revision

Revision is part of writing. The editor gives you natural language — you
diagnose and fix. Never wait for a numbered instruction list; never invent a
technique id to apply mechanically.

## 1. Diagnose the feedback

- Restate the feedback in concrete terms before touching the draft. "太像
  AI" usually means some combination of: template sentences, uniform rhythm,
  no concrete anchors, abstract nouns doing the work, people who do not act.
- Map each complaint to a place in the draft. Find the worst instances first;
  fixing the worst three is better than smoothing all twenty.

## 2. Re-observe before revising

- Re-read the material for what the feedback is asking ('人物没出来' → find
  the person's acts/speech/waits in the material; '开头太像背景说明' → find a
  real scene to enter through).
- Consult the relevant skill if your draft still flattens scenes
  (scene-preserving-writing) or ends in summaries (longform-feature-writing).

## 3. Edit surgically

- Preserve every fact, number, quote and claim. Never rewrite a fact to make
  the prose smoother.
- Prefer local repairs: replace a summary opener with a scene, cut a template
  sentence, restore an ignored detail, break a monotone paragraph's rhythm.
- If a structural problem is bigger than local fixes (e.g. the whole opening
  is wrong), restructure that section and say why in your summary — do not
  silently rewrite the entire piece.

## 4. Verify the revision

- After revising, re-check claims against the material before saving: every
  fact still traceable, nothing newly invented, quotes still verbatim.
- Pass the full source ledger with save_artifact and let ACE validate.

## 5. Respond to the feedback, not to a checklist

- The human editor's words are the requirement. If the feedback says
  "判断句太多", your revision must measurably reduce judgment sentences — not
  sprinkle in one observation and call it done.
